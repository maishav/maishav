My Story

From Bhola's coast to the economics of everyday life.

I grew up in Bhola — a coastal district in southern Bangladesh where the rhythms of life are shaped by the river, the tide, and the ever-present pressures of climate and economy. That background gave me an instinct for the questions that development economics tries to answer: How do households cope when the harvest fails? What does "food secure" actually feel like? Why do some communities manage their environment sustainably and others don't?

I pursued those questions formally at the University of Barishal, graduating with a BSS in Economics (GPA 3.69/4) in August 2025. Over five years of study, I built a toolkit that combines rigorous econometrics — STATA, regression analysis, panel data — with qualitative depth through NVivo thematic coding and structured field surveys.

My two primary research projects — a KAP study of waste management across 431 coastal households, and a food security study across 400 households in coastal Bhola — are currently under review for peer-reviewed publication. I also contributed data collection to a published study on HPV awareness (Heliyon, DOI: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e30897).

Beyond academic research, I worked as a Business Economics Researcher at Innovision Consulting in Dhaka, where I assessed microeconomic impacts on youth-led enterprises, produced analytical briefs, and facilitated stakeholder consultations. That experience confirmed what my field surveys had taught me: good research only matters if it reaches the people who can act on it.

I am now preparing graduate school applications and actively seeking research analyst roles where I can continue building at the intersection of economics, environment, and development policy.
