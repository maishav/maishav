My Story

From Bhola's coast to the economics of everyday life.

I grew up in Bhola — a coastal district in southern Bangladesh where the rhythms of life are shaped by the river, the tide, and the ever-present pressures of climate and economy. That background gave me an instinct for the questions that development economics tries to answer: why some households recover from a bad harvest while their neighbours don't, what "food secure" actually feels like day to day, and why some communities manage shared resources sustainably and others don't.

I pursued those questions formally at the University of Barishal, graduating with a BSS in Economics (GPA 3.69/4) in August 2025. Over five years of study, I built a toolkit that combines rigorous econometrics — STATA, regression analysis, panel data — with qualitative depth through NVivo thematic coding and structured field surveys.

My two primary research projects — a KAP study of waste management across 431 coastal households, and a food security study across 400 households in coastal Bhola — are currently under review for peer-reviewed publication. I also contributed data collection to a published study on HPV awareness (Heliyon, DOI: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e30897).

Beyond academic research, I interned as an Economics Researcher at Innovision Consulting in Dhaka, where I evaluated microeconomic impacts of a UNDP-funded programme on youth-led enterprises, produced analytical briefs, and facilitated stakeholder consultations. That experience confirmed what my field surveys had taught me: good research only matters if it reaches the people who can act on it.

I'm now interested in graduate programmes in development or environmental economics, and looking for research analyst roles at the intersection of economics, environment, and policy.
